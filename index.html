01 · About

One traceable picture of the world's plastic.

Plastic Atlas is a source-first data service on global plastics, developed by the From Waste to Resource (W2R) lab at the Technion. It brings trusted international datasets together in one place and keeps every number traceable to its source.

The problem it solves

SCATTERED

Many sources, no single view

Figures on production, use, waste and pollution come from different organisations and are rarely shown side by side.

INCOMPARABLE

Different measures look alike

Production, use and waste are related but different quantities. Mixing them up leads to misleading comparisons.

UNTRACEABLE

Numbers lose their origin

Once a figure is quoted and re-quoted, its year, method and whether it was measured or modelled are often lost.

How it works

The atlas does not produce its own estimates. It collects, labels and connects data published by international organisations and peer-reviewed research (see Sources), and follows plastic as a material flow: what it is made of, what it is used for, how it becomes waste and where that waste goes.

No value is stored without the information needed to check it.

sourceThe organisation and publication the value comes from.
versionWhich release or edition of that source was used.
unitWhat is counted, e.g. million tonnes (Mt) or % share.
geographyWorld, region, country or city.
methodHow the source produced the value.
statusReported (measured) or modelled (estimated).

06 · Time

From 2 Mt to 1,231 Mt.

Historical global plastics production was about 2 Mt in 1950. OECD estimates global plastics use at 460 Mt in 2019 and projects 1,231 Mt in 2060 under its baseline scenario. Production and use are related but not identical measures.
